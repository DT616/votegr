Privacy by design

How it works

We created an engine that runs locally in your browser that finds your voter information based on where you live, and generates directions to your polling place while avoiding license plate cameras. The data files come down once, when you first visit. Everything after that happens on your device: the lookup and the directions are worked out against those files, so your address never leaves your browser. There is no geocoder to call, no routing server to ask, and nothing on the page reporting what you looked up. Once it has loaded it makes no requests at all, to us or to anyone else, and it keeps working with no connection.

It covers every city and township in Kent County: 30 jurisdictions and 202 precincts, loaded once and held on your device, so a lookup or a route anywhere in the county asks nothing further of the network. Your ward is shown where your city has wards; most townships do not. Grand Rapids' polling places, early voting sites and drop boxes come from the City Clerk; every other jurisdiction's polling places, drop boxes and clerk's office come from the Kent County Clerk's pages.

The files this page uses to compute your information were compiled from published city, state and county data sources. The camera locations come from the data DeFlock publishes on OpenStreetMap for projects like this to use. That sync happens when the site is built, not while you are reading it, so the list is as current as the last publish rather than as current as this minute.

What this does not hide

A route around the plate readers is a route around the ones volunteers have mapped, and nothing more. Newly installed and covert readers are not in the data, a camera watches a corridor rather than a point, and the roads themselves are watched in ways no list covers.

The drop boxes are the plainest case, and worth saying outright on a page that argues for privacy: every absentee ballot drop box is under video surveillance. That is not an accident of where they sit, it is MCL 168.761d, which requires the clerk to monitor each one. The statute says video monitoring does not include storing the video, but do not read that as a promise about any particular box. Voting in person, voting early and posting a ballot are all watched at the far end; what this page can keep private is the lookup and the journey.

Only six of the county's thirty jurisdictions publish a drop box at all. Where yours does not, this page sends you to your own clerk's office, never to a neighbouring city's box, because under MCL 168.764a an absentee ballot can be returned only to the clerk of the city or township where you are registered.

Why make this?

  1. The state's Michigan Voter Information Center asks for your name, your birth month and year and your ZIP code before it will show you your ward and precinct, and every lookup runs on its servers. The notice you agree to on that form says, in full:
    The information collected on this form is only what is needed to complete your transaction as authorized by MCL 168.509ii, MCL 168.759, MCL 168.759a, and MCL 168.764c. As a public body, MDOS is subject to the Michigan Freedom of Information Act (FOIA), MCL 15.231 et seq., and information such as a name or address may be disclosed in response to a FOIA request.
    This site is a proof of concept that none of that information needs to be collected to show you where you vote. Every answer here comes from public records already on your device; there is no form, no transaction, and nothing for a FOIA request to disclose, because nothing was ever collected.
  2. Raise awareness that decentralized, free and open source software is possible, and we should foster more development and creativity to solve problems using principles that provide utility, maximize privacy and decentralization.
  3. Raise awareness about license plate cameras and how they might impact even the sacred act of voting. Nobody should have a reason to feel uneasy about being tracked to go vote. While being pulled over because a camera read your license plate is unlikely for most, it is a real possibility, as are false positives. If this site helps one eligible voter find the polls and vote, and results in the government having less data to collect, it was worth it.
