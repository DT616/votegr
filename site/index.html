Privacy by design

How it works

Everything runs in your browser. The data files, every street, precinct, polling place and camera in Kent County, come down once when the page loads; the lookup and the directions are then worked out on your device against those files. Your address never leaves your browser, nothing reports what you looked up, and after loading the page makes no further requests to anyone, so it keeps working if your connection drops while it is open.

Coverage is all 30 cities and townships in Kent County, 202 precincts. Wards are shown for the five cities that have them (Grand Rapids, Wyoming, Kentwood, Walker and East Grand Rapids); townships have none. Grand Rapids' polling places, early voting sites and drop boxes come from the City Clerk; everywhere else, polling places, drop boxes and the clerk's office come from the Kent County Clerk. Camera locations are the license plate readers mapped on OpenStreetMap by the DeFlock community, refreshed daily when the site is built: as current as the last refresh, not this minute.

Why make this?

  1. The state's Michigan Voter Information Center asks for your name, your birth month and year and your ZIP code before it will show you your ward and precinct, and every lookup runs on its servers. The notice you agree to on that form says, in full:
    The information collected on this form is only what is needed to complete your transaction as authorized by MCL 168.509ii, MCL 168.759, MCL 168.759a, and MCL 168.764c. As a public body, MDOS is subject to the Michigan Freedom of Information Act (FOIA), MCL 15.231 et seq., and information such as a name or address may be disclosed in response to a FOIA request.
    This site is a proof of concept that none of that has to happen. Nothing about your registration needs to be processed on the state's servers to show you where you vote, and the state does not need to learn that you looked. Your voter record is already a public record, disclosable under FOIA whether or not you ever agree to that notice -- so the notice is not protecting the record. It is asking you to consent to a new one: that you, from this address, on this day, looked up your own information. Why should finding out where you vote create another data point about you, one you have to agree to share before the tool will work? Every answer here comes from public records already on your device. There is no form to agree to, and no record of the lookup exists anywhere, because it never left your hands.
  2. Raise awareness that decentralized, free and open source software is possible, and we should foster more development and creativity to solve problems using principles that provide utility, maximize privacy and decentralization.
  3. Raise awareness about license plate cameras and how they might impact even the sacred act of voting. Nobody should have a reason to feel uneasy about being tracked to go vote. While being pulled over because a camera read your license plate is unlikely for most, it is a real possibility, as are false positives. If this site helps one eligible voter find the polls and vote, and results in the government having less data to collect, it was worth it.
